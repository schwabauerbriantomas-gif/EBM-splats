Subject: Empirical observation: energy-guided steering on LLaDA-8B (extends Diffusion-LM to 8B scale)

Dear Shen Nie and Chongxuan Liu,

I am writing to share an empirical observation from experiments with
LLaDA-8B-Instruct. The intent is purely informational.

I applied logit-level guidance at each denoising step of LLaDA-8B to steer
generation toward semantic targets. This technique was established by
Diffusion-LM (Li & Liang, ACL 2022) as a controllable generation method.
My contribution is simply to characterize how it behaves on a modern 8B-scale
instruction-tuned masked diffusion model — something that, to my knowledge,
has not been empirically documented yet.

What I observed:

- Pre-computed energy vectors (derived from target text via the model's own
  embedding matrix) injected into logits at each step produced a 39% increase
  in semantic similarity to the target topic over baseline, while maintaining
  output coherence.

- The approach I used differs from Diffusion-LM in that it requires no
  gradient computation. A single matrix-vector product (embedding matrix x
  target direction) produces per-token energy scores. This makes the
  injection computationally cheap: one mv() call per denoising step.

- Effectiveness is topic-dependent. Distinctive vocabulary (horror:
  target_sim 0.44, ocean: 0.34) is steered effectively. Common vocabulary
  that competes with strong model priors (cooking: 0.07, space: 0.18)
  resists steering.

- There is a narrow effective range for the injection strength parameter
  (alpha 8-12). Below that, the effect is negligible; above it, outputs
  collapse into degenerate repetition.

- The fundamental limitation: logit-level injection steers vocabulary
  selection but does not alter narrative planning. All outputs retained the
  model's default story template regardless of guidance target.

From a security perspective, the observation is that masked diffusion models
expose N injection points per output (one per denoising step, typically 128)
vs 1 per token in autoregressive models. This is a structural property of
the architecture, not a bug. I want to be clear: this requires white-box
access (model weights and embedding matrix). It is not a server-side
exploit and does not affect black-box API users.
